Under the threshold

Not regulated

No permit needed to remove a tree from an existing home in Hutto.

Hutto's clearing and tree removal permit only applies to undeveloped land, a redevelopment site, or a developed non-residential lot — not an existing house. Removing a tree from your own developed residential lot doesn't need a permit at all. One thing worth knowing before you cut: if the tree is a healthy, non-nuisance species at 2.5 inches diameter or more, the code asks for 1-to-1 inch replacement — plant back what you take out — rather than a permit. Dead, diseased, dangerous, or a listed nuisance species (hackberry, Arizona ash, cedar, Bradford pear, mesquite, chinaberry and others) comes out with nothing owed at all.

The ordinance, in its own words

A clearing and tree removal permit is required for removal of trees with a DBH of at least 6 in. on an undeveloped or redevelopment site, or at least 2.5 in. on a developed non-residential site, regardless of health. Healthy native tall and small trees with a DBH of 2.5 in. or more that cannot be considered diseased, dangerous or dead may be removed if the gross DBH loss is replaced 1:1.

Source: City of Hutto — Unified Development Code § 10.407.8, Tree Preservation and Removal (revised March 2024). This is a reading of the code, not legal advice and not the city’s own determination. Ordinances change.

Everything else the Hutto code attaches

If it's part of building or development
The permit itself only applies to undeveloped land, a redevelopment site (6 inches DBH), or a developed non-residential lot (2.5 inches DBH) — not an existing house.
How it's measured
DBH. Permit trigger: 6 inches on undeveloped/redevelopment land, 2.5 inches on developed non-residential. Residential replacement trigger: 2.5 inches.
If it's your own home
A healthy, non-nuisance tree at 2.5 inches diameter or more on a developed residential lot asks for 1-to-1 diameter-inch replacement, not a permit.
What's exempt
Dead, diseased, dangerous, or nuisance-species trees (hackberry, Arizona ash, cedar, Bradford pear, mesquite, chinaberry, among others) can come out any time — no permit, no replacement.
Where it's written
Unified Development Code § 10.407.8, Tree Preservation and Removal.

Healthy native tall and… That is City of Hutto — Unified Development Code § 10.407.8, Tree Preservation and Removal (revised March 2024), and it is quoted in full further down this page.

How do I measure my tree for Hutto's rules?

Put a tape around the trunk 4.5 feet above the ground to get the circumference, then divide by 3.14 for the diameter. Hutto writes its rule in inches in diameter. If the trunk splits into several stems below 4.5 feet, measure below the split — that usually puts you in a higher bracket than you expected.

What if the tree is dead or dangerous?

Most ordinances around here treat a dead, diseased or genuinely hazardous tree differently from a healthy one somebody would simply rather not have, and a written assessment from a Certified Arborist is usually what the city wants to see. There is one on my crew. Get the condition documented before you file anything, not after.
