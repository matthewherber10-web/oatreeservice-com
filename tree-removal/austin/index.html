What Austin actually regulates

Austin runs city arborist review and permitting with its own protected and heritage tiers, and it's the one place where getting the sequence wrong is genuinely expensive. Beyond the rules: east Austin sits on black prairie clay, west Austin sits on limestone, and the same species behaves differently on either side of MoPac.

24 inches in diameter and up

The most protected category

Heritage tree — the most protected category, and still not a dead end.

At 24 inches or more, on the listed species, Austin classes this as a Heritage tree. Removal is prohibited except as provided under City Code section 25-8-641, which does set out routes through it. A tree that is dead, diseased or an imminent hazard also runs on a separate track entirely. This is a real process rather than a form you fill in, so worth talking through before you assume the answer is no.

19 inches in diameter and up

Permit needed

Protected tree. You need a permit to remove it.

At 19 inches or more, any species is a Protected tree in Austin and a permit is required to remove it.

Under the threshold

Not regulated

Under 19 inches on residential property, the city doesn't regulate it.

Austin's tree ordinance doesn't reach trees under 19 inches in diameter on residential property, so no Tree Ordinance Review Application is needed. An HOA or plat restriction still can.

The ordinance, in its own words

A Protected tree has a diameter of 19 inches or more, measured 4.5 feet above the ground. A Heritage tree has a diameter of 24 inches or more and is one of the listed species; removal of a heritage tree is prohibited, except as provided under Section 25-8-641.

Source: City of Austin — Tree Reviews and Permitting. This is a reading of the code, not legal advice and not the city’s own determination. Ordinances change.

Everything else the Austin code attaches

If it's not construction-related
A removal not tied to construction goes through a Tree Ordinance Review Application (TORA).
If it's part of building or development
Where there's development, tree review runs concurrently with building plan review and the tree permit accompanies the residential building permit.
Species rules
The Heritage list is all oaks, pecan, American elm, cedar elm, bald cypress, Texas ash, Texas madrone, bigtooth maple, Arizona walnut and eastern black walnut. At 24 inches those are effectively unremovable without meeting 25-8-641.
How it's measured
Diameter measured 4.5 feet above the ground. Three tiers on private property: 8 to 18 inches, Protected at 19, Heritage at 24.
Dead, diseased or hazardous trees
Austin runs a separate track for dead, diseased and imminent-hazard trees. It's a process, not a free pass, but it's the right door to knock on.
Who to call
Austin Development Services, City Arborist Program

The trees I get called about here

It depends entirely on which side of town you're on — east Austin's black clay grows pecan, cedar elm and hackberry; west Austin's limestone grows live oak and ashe juniper. Citywide, the two things I'm called about most are oak wilt, which is well established in Travis County, and limb failure on mature live oaks. The February 2023 ice storm did enormous damage to Austin's canopy and a lot of what I still see are trees carrying broken, hung-up or badly torn limbs from it.

How do I measure my tree for Austin's rules?

Put a tape around the trunk 4.5 feet above the ground to get the circumference, then divide by 3.14 for the diameter. Austin writes its rule in inches in diameter. If the trunk splits into several stems below 4.5 feet, measure below the split — that usually puts you in a higher bracket than you expected.

What if the tree is dead or dangerous?

Austin runs a separate track for dead, diseased and imminent-hazard trees. It's a process, not a free pass, but it's the right door to knock on.
