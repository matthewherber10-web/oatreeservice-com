Settle the argument

Do you need a permit to remove your tree?

Answer three questions and I'll show you what your city's ordinance actually says, in its own words, with a link to the code.

Before you start

You need one measurement: the circumference of the trunk at 4.5 feet above the ground. A tape measure and thirty seconds. If the trunk splits into several stems below that height, measure below the split — that usually puts you in a higher bracket than you expected.

Most people measure around the trunk. I'll do the conversion.

What this tool is, and what it isn't

It's a lookup of ordinance text I have read, for the four cities I have read it for. It shows you the wording and the source so you can check me. It is not legal advice and it is not the city's determination — only the city can give you that, and the person who pays a mitigation assessment is the property owner.

I built it because this argument comes up constantly on jobs, and both sides are usually guessing. Guessing is what gets a heritage oak cut down and a bill sent afterwards.

Why only four cities

Because I've read four ordinances end to end. I work in {len(TOWNS)} towns, and I'm not going to encode a rule I haven't verified into a tool that people will act on. For the rest, the tool gives you the two questions to ask your city, which takes one phone call and settles it properly.

I'll add cities as I verify them. If you've been through the process somewhere I haven't covered, tell me what you found and I'll check it and add it.

Measuring, properly

Every one of these ordinances is written in inches measured 4.5 feet above the ground — foresters call it DBH, diameter at breast height. Put a tape around the trunk at that height, and divide the circumference by 3.14 to get diameter. Sixty inches around is roughly nineteen across, which is precisely where Austin's rules begin.

Two things that trip people up: measure below the split if the trunk divides under 4.5 feet, and remember that "caliper inches" and "diameter inches" aren't quite the same term in every code, though at this size they're used interchangeably in the ordinances here.
